Token rewards for contributors

How an open-source contribution to a DeSciX community becomes tokens in the contributor's wallet: bounty, pull request, award, monthly ledger, offer, acceptance in Powch, settlement. The worked example is DeSciX/egpt-godsworld, whose community token is EGPT. As built in daita-beast 1.6.4, checked against the scripts on 2026-10-21; the DeSciX platform's side was read from its source the same day. At 23:13 UTC that day the marketplace DeSciX/DAITA pinned daita-beast ref "1.6.4" (since its commit 60c51f4, 20:41 UTC), and tag 1.6.4 of DeSciX/daita-beast was commit 3eacea0; so a marketplace install was 1.6.4 at that time.

In short
  • A contributor installs nothing. Find a bounty on the public repo, send a pull request, and the award arrives as a comment on it. To receive the tokens you need a DeSciX account whose verified email is the one the community owner has for your GitHub login.
  • An award is a GitHub comment plus a label. A repo admin or maintainer posts one comment of a fixed shape, BEAST-AWARD amount=… token=… to=@… for="…", and adds the label awarded. Nothing else records it.
  • The token is the community's, and the community decides who awards it. For egpt-godsworld that is EGPT. The community repo DeSciX/EGPT commits .beast/awarders.json, which lists DeSciX/egpt-godsworld among the repos that may award EGPT.
  • The platform pays. The community owner turns the month's ledger into a sheet (ledger.sh --sheet), and a DeSciX platform operator imports that sheet as offers; the sheet is the import's only input. The recipient accepts the offer in Powch, which binds a wallet, and the tokens are minted in a staged batch that settles the offer. Beast itself touches no chain and no wallet.
  • One fact Beast cannot supply: the email of each awarded GitHub login. The sheet's email column is where it enters, and the community owner fills it from a login→email file they build by hand (Gap 1).
  • Whose GitHub identity and quota each call uses is covered in Beast messaging paths.

1. The path

Bounty issue
label bounty→ Your PR
Closes #n or Bounty #n→ Merge (or decline)
a maintainer, in GitHub→ Award
BEAST-AWARD + awarded→ Monthly ledger
ledger.sh→ Offers sheet
ledger.sh --sheet, the community owner's→ Offers import
a platform operator→ Offer email
link to /offer/<id>→ Accept in Powch
binds your wallet→ Settle
staged batch minted

A bounty is optional. A merged PR without a bounty, a review that led to a change, or a good-effort submission that was declined can all be awarded at the owner's discretion, as the repo's award policy says.

2. For a contributor: one bounty on egpt-godsworld, end to end

Real: DeSciX/egpt-godsworld and DeSciX/EGPT (both public). Invented: carol-dev, an outside developer, and the bounty and PR numbers.

  1. Find a bounty. Open issues labelled bounty on the public repo: https://github.com/DeSciX/egpt-godsworld/issues?q=is%3Aopen+label%3Abounty. The issue's ## Bounty section gives the amount, which is a floor, not a cap. On 2026-10-21 at 19:37 UTC the repo had no bounty issue, open or closed (Gap 6).
  2. Submit a pull request on that repo, one submission per PR, with Closes #<n> or Bounty #<n> in the description. You need no Beast install, no seat and no DeSciX account to submit.
  3. Wait for the maintainer's decision. If accepted, the PR is merged and you receive the award. A declined PR is closed and may still receive an effort award.
  4. The award arrives as a comment. GitHub notifies you, because the comment mentions @carol-dev. Only a comment from a repo admin or maintainer counts. Anyone else can post the same text, and the ledger drops it.
  5. Tell the community owner which email to use. The offer is addressed to an email, not to a GitHub login, and you can accept it only from a DeSciX account whose verified email is that address. How the owner learns it is not built into Beast or the platform (Gap 1).
  6. Check the ledger. Each month the community owner commits awards/<YYYY-MM>.md to DeSciX/EGPT. It lists totals per login and every award.
  7. Accept the offer. After a platform operator imports the month's offers sheet, the platform emails you an offer with a link to /offer/<offer_id>. The offer expires 30 days after it is written. Open the link, sign in to DeSciX with Powch, choose a wallet and sign. Powch signs the exact text OFFER-ACCEPT <offer_id> <wallet_address> and the platform checks the signature, the offer's email against your account's verified email, and the expiry. Acceptance binds the wallet and records a pending claim. If your account already has a verified email, a registered wallet and KYC compliance in the community when the offer is imported, the platform's auto-claim claims it for you to that wallet, with no acceptance step (section 4).
  8. Receive the tokens. A platform admin stages every pending, wallet-bound claim of the token into one batch, and the token's minter signs the mint with their own wallet. A claim whose owner is not KYC-compliant in the community (a Stripe verification stamp, or a KYC mark whose signer still holds a KYC role; section 4) is held back and stages on a later run. When the batch's mint succeeds, the offer is settled and carries the mint's transaction hash.

3. For maintainers: the policy, bounties, awards and the ledger

The award policy (once per public repo)

bounty.sh and award.sh read .beast/award-policy.md from the root of the checkout you run them in. They refuse to run unless the file meets four conditions:

  • Its first line names the repo you are awarding on.
  • It has a Token: line, and that token matches the one you pass.
  • It has a Community: <owner/repo> line naming the repo that holds the token's community.
  • It contains no unfilled field.

Awarding on DeSciX/egpt-godsworld runs from a checkout of its private twin (Setting up Beast, section 4). Write the policy there with init-repo.sh, naming the public repo:

${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/init-repo.sh DeSciX/egpt-godsworld --path ~/code/egpt-godsworld-twin --token EGPT --community DeSciX/EGPT

--token and --community go together; one without the other is refused. Because DeSciX/egpt-godsworld is public, init-repo.sh runs in its public mode: on the public repo it creates only the bounty and awarded labels; into the checkout it writes the bounty issue template and the policy; it refuses on stderr every coordination label, the decision and workstream templates and the roster. It deletes nothing, and when the public repo already carries a coordination label it names each one and exits 1. On 2026-10-21 at 19:37 UTC DeSciX/egpt-godsworld's labels were bounty, awarded and GitHub's defaults, so that refusal does not apply there. The policy it writes begins:

# Award policy — DeSciX/egpt-godsworld

Token: EGPT (awarded amounts reach holders as offers: ledger.sh --sheet, then the platform's offers import).
Community: DeSciX/EGPT (the repo that holds the EGPT community: its `.beast/awarders.json` lists the repos that may award EGPT; a repo it does not list awards nothing).

That Token: line is the template's at tag 1.6.4 (plugin/templates/award-policy.md:3). The template at tag 1.6.3 and before writes Token: EGPT (conceptual units until the DeSciX DIP import exists). instead, a clause that predates the Offers import of section 4 (Gap 5). Publish the policy and the bounty template so contributors can read what each bounty cites: publish.sh prepare award-policy --paths .beast/award-policy.md .github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/bounty.md ….

Which repos may award the token

The community decides. Its repo, the one each policy names on its Community: line, commits .beast/awarders.json on its default branch. For EGPT that repo is DeSciX/EGPT, which added the file in commit 75621c2 (2026-10-20 20:30 UTC). Read on 2026-10-21 at 19:37 UTC with gh api 'repos/DeSciX/EGPT/contents/.beast/awarders.json?ref=75621c2' --jq .content | base64 -d, the file is:

{"token": "EGPT", "awarders": ["DeSciX/EGPT", "DeSciX/egpt-frqtl", "DeSciX/egpt-godsworld", "DeSciX/egpt-lean"]}

The same read on the default branch (main at 0520876) returned the same blob, f41b8a2; 75621c2 is the only commit that touches the file. The file has exactly the keys token and awarders (a non-empty list of owner/repo, each once, compared case-insensitively), and it is read through GitHub, never from a local copy. award.sh and bounty.sh refuse a repo it does not list, naming the community repo and the missing entry (refused: <repo> may not award EGPT. The community repo DeSciX/EGPT lists the repos that may award EGPT in .beast/awarders.json (…)). A community repo without the file refuses every award and bounty of its token. A public ledger checks the same list for every repo in --repos. To let another repo award EGPT, the owner of DeSciX/EGPT adds it to awarders; that repo's own policy cannot grant it.

DeSciX/EGPT carries no LICENSE.md at its root (404 on 2026-10-21). It licenses per directory: thirteen paths carry their own license file, among them 00_OUTPUTS/LICENSE.md, 13_MAXI/LICENSE.md, microservice/LICENSE.md and plugins/egpt/LICENSE.md. A contribution there falls under the license of the directory it changes.

Post a bounty

${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/bounty.sh --repo DeSciX/egpt-godsworld --title "A Three.js orbit-camera scene for Maxi" --amount 500 --token EGPT \
  --what "A scene Maxi can load, with orbit and pan controls from Three.js OrbitControls. Accepted means: merged, with a short demo in the PR."

This creates an issue titled Bounty: A Three.js orbit-camera scene for Maxi with the label bounty and the sections Bounty, What, How to submit and Decides. The Bounty section reads 500 EGPT for the accepted submission; effort awards at the owner's discretion (see .beast/award-policy.md). Bounties are allowed on a public repo: unlike decisions and status, they are not coordination writes.

Award

${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/award.sh DeSciX/egpt-godsworld#12 --amount 500 --token EGPT --to @carol-dev \
  --for "accepted orbit-camera scene (PR #12)" --ref https://github.com/DeSciX/egpt-godsworld/issues/7

This posts exactly one comment and adds the awarded label:

BEAST-AWARD amount=500 token=EGPT to=@carol-dev for="accepted orbit-camera scene (PR #12)" ref=https://github.com/DeSciX/egpt-godsworld/issues/7

Without --ref the comment carries no ref=, and the ledger cites the awarded item's URL instead. The amount is a plain decimal in token units: 500, 12.5 and 0.25 are accepted. The script refuses when any of these holds:

  • Your role on the repo is not admin or maintain.
  • The amount is not a plain decimal (no thousands separator, no sign, no leading zero, no trailing zero after the point).
  • The token is not 2–12 upper-case letters or digits.
  • The recipient is not a GitHub login.
  • --for contains a double quote.
  • --ref is given and is not an https URL free of spaces, double quotes, < and >.
  • The policy is missing, names another repo or another token, or has no Community: line.
  • The community repo's .beast/awarders.json is missing or does not list the repo.

The moderator agent can draft a suggested amount as a MODERATOR-DRAFT comment, but it never awards, merges or labels awarded.

Monthly ledger

${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/ledger.sh --community EGPT --repos DeSciX/egpt-godsworld,DeSciX/egpt-frqtl --month 2026-11 --out ~/code/EGPT/awards
git -C ~/code/EGPT add awards/2026-11.jsonl awards/2026-11.md && git -C ~/code/EGPT commit -m "Awards 2026-11"

It reads every awarded issue and PR in the listed repos and keeps a BEAST-AWARD comment only when all five of these hold:

  • The comment parses.
  • Its amount is a plain decimal, as award.sh requires.
  • It was made in that month (UTC).
  • Its token equals --community.
  • Its author is an admin or maintainer of that repo, checked live.

Each dropped comment is printed as DROPPED <url>: <reason>, and the run exits 2. A ledger with holes is never published by accident. Never edit the files by hand: award and regenerate.

Every row's ref is the award's own ref=, or the awarded item's URL when the comment has none, so no row has an empty ref. A ref= that is present but empty or not an https URL stops the run with exit 1 before any file is written, naming the item, the author and the value. The check runs before the month and token filters, so a malformed ref= on any awarded item of the listed repos stops the ledger of every month. Edit the comment to a full ref=<https url>, or remove ref=, and rerun.

Two more checks stop the run instead of dropping anything:

  • A public ledger lists only public repos the community lists. When --out is inside a checkout of a public or public-intended repo (here DeSciX/EGPT), every repo in --repos must be public or public-intended too, and listed in that repo's .beast/awarders.json for --community. Either failure is refused, naming the repo, before anything is read. A private repo's awards stay in a private ledger, with --out in the private repo's own checkout.
  • The role read needs push access. GitHub answers a role query only to a login with push access on the repo. When the read fails (a 403), the run stops with exit 1 before writing any ledger file, naming the repo, the login and GitHub's answer, and no award is dropped for it. So the community owner runs the ledger with a login that has push access on every listed repo.

The .jsonl rows (repo, item, to, token, amount, for, ref, by, at) are the machine-readable ledger, and each amount is the award's own decimal. The .md file lists totals per login and every award.

4. From ledger to tokens

The login→email map

An award names a GitHub login; a DeSciX offer is addressed to an email. The mapping between them is the platform's data, and no Beast script or platform command produces it: the community owner keeps it as a file outside every repo, a CSV whose first line is the header login,email and every other line <login>,<email>, with no quoting and logins compared case-insensitively.

The offers sheet: ledger.sh --sheet

${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/scripts/ledger.sh --community EGPT --repos DeSciX/egpt-godsworld,DeSciX/egpt-frqtl --month 2026-11 --out ~/code/EGPT/awards \
  --sheet ~/descix-offers/egpt-2026-11.csv --emails ~/descix-offers/emails.csv

This writes the month's award rows, the same rows as the .jsonl, as the CSV the platform's Offers import reads. The sheet is the import's only input; the import never reads the .jsonl. The sheet:

  • the header contactemail,token,new_amount,batch,wallet_address, then one row per award;
  • contactemail the recipient's email from the map, token the award's token, new_amount its amount as the award wrote it;
  • batch awards-<YYYY-MM> unless --batch names another (a letter or digit first, then letters, digits, ., _, -; at most 64 in all);
  • wallet_address always blank, because the wallet binds when the offer is accepted in Powch;
  • RFC 4180 quoting and LF line ends.

--sheet and --emails go together. A map without the header, with a line that is not one login and one email, with a login that is not a GitHub login, with a malformed email, with a login listed twice, or with no mapping at all dies naming the file and the line, before GitHub is read. A --sheet that is a directory or a symbolic link, or whose directory does not exist, is refused before anything is read too. An awarded login the map lacks refuses the whole run, naming every unmapped login, and nothing is written: no sheet, no .jsonl, no .md. The sheet carries email addresses, so a --sheet path inside any git checkout, ignored or not, is refused before anything is read. A run that drops a comment still writes the sheet without it, and exits 2.

The Offers import

The import is an operator step on the DeSciX platform: a platform admin runs it, and there is no command a community owner calls. The import takes a Google Sheet by its URL (airdrop_load_sheet's sheet_url), while ledger.sh --sheet writes a CSV file; loading that CSV into a Google Sheet is the community owner's step. The import reads one source, that sheet, and refuses any other source by name: UNKNOWN_IMPORT_SOURCE, "a sheet is the only offer source". It reads the sheet through the platform-admin command airdrop_load_sheet, whose reader (readAirdropSheet, then parseSheetData) takes the sheet's first worksheet and matches columns by header name, trimmed and case-insensitive (AIRDROP_SHEET_WORKSHEET, AIRDROP_SHEET_MATCH). A row with neither email nor wallet is refused by parseSheetData (ROW_NO_WALLET_NO_EMAIL). importOffers then turns each row into one offer in the token's community, in the state offered, addressed to the row's contactemail. The offer records the email, the token, the amount, the batch label and the sheet's URL as its ref; it carries no GitHub login, award ref or awarder. Its identity is a hash of the sheet's URL, the email (lower-cased), the token and the amount.

A row is refused by name, and the rest are still imported, when its email is not a mailable address, its ref is empty, its token is not a registered token (UNKNOWN_TOKEN), the token's community does not exist in that environment (COMMUNITY_NOT_MATERIALIZED), or its amount has more decimal places than the token has. Importing the same row again writes nothing, because the offer's identity is the same. Equal awards can therefore collapse into one offer (Gap 2).

The offer email, acceptance, staging and settlement

  1. Email. A platform admin has the platform send each offer to its email, with the accept link <platform origin>/offer/<offer_id>. An offer expires 30 days after it is written.
  2. Accept in Powch. The link opens Powch on the offer. The recipient picks a wallet, Powch signs OFFER-ACCEPT <offer_id> <wallet_address>, and the platform command accept_offer first checks that the signature was made by that wallet over that text, then, in one transaction, that the offer is still offered and unexpired and that its email is the verified email of the signed-in account. The same transaction binds the account and the wallet to the offer, records a pending claim for the amount, and moves the offer to claimed. Each failed check is refused by name: OFFER_BAD_SIGNATURE, OFFER_NOT_OFFERED, OFFER_EXPIRED, OFFER_EMAIL_MISMATCH.
  3. Auto-claim. After writing the rows, importOffers runs the auto-claim sweep (autoClaimForImportedEmails, then autoClaimOffersForUser's transition): for an account whose verified email is the offer's, with a registered wallet and KYC compliance in the community, it claims the offer to that wallet itself, through the same transition as an acceptance. Read in source, not run here.
  4. Stage. A platform admin stages every pending, wallet-bound claim of the token as one batch. A claim whose account is not KYC-compliant in the community is held and stays pending; when every candidate is held, nothing is staged (ALL_CLAIMS_KYC_UNVERIFIED, from stageBatchFromClaims through holdKycUnverified). KYC compliance is one predicate, User.isKycCompliant over User.kycAuthority: a Stripe verification stamp on the account, or a KYC mark for that community whose signer still holds a KYC role on the community's token when the check runs. Read in source, not run here.
  5. Mint and settle. The token's minter executes the staged batch, signing the mint with their own wallet; the platform has no server-side mint for it. When the mint's receipt is a success, each claim of the batch is fulfilled and each offer moves to settled, carrying the mint's transaction hash.

Gaps: what 1.6.4 and the platform do not do

  1. The GitHub login → email mapping has no owner that produces it. An offer is addressed to the sheet row's email, and acceptance checks that email against the account's verified email. Neither Beast nor the platform maps a GitHub login to an account or an email: the community owner builds the login,email file by hand, and ledger.sh --sheet copies each award's email from it into the sheet's contactemail column, refusing the whole run when an awarded login is missing (logins compared case-insensitively). A wrong email in that file reaches the offer unchecked; the offer then waits for an account with that email verified.
  2. Equal awards can collapse into one offer. A platform defect, read in source, not run here, and reported to the platform: an offer's identity hashes the sheet's URL, the email, the token and the amount, and a second row with the same identity writes nothing. Neither the batch label nor the row's position is part of it, so two awards of the same amount and token to one recipient in one month's sheet become one offer, and the recipient is offered half. Until that changes, the community owner checks a month's sheet for rows that repeat email, token and amount.
  3. Awarding needs a role on the repo the comment lands on. award.sh refuses unless the caller's role is admin or maintain (plugin/scripts/award.sh:35-37). Three.js maintainers cannot award on DeSciX/* repos without a grant, and DeSciX cannot award on mrdoob/three.js. GitHub's documented permission levels give a personal account's repo only an owner and write collaborators, with no maintain role, so on mrdoob/three.js only mrdoob could award. That last point comes from GitHub's documentation and was not measured here.
  4. Another organization's token has no DeSciX home. award.sh accepts any 2–12 character symbol (plugin/scripts/award.sh:27), so with its own community repo and .beast/awarders.json a THREE ledger can be generated. The platform's import refuses a token that is not registered and a community that does not exist, so nothing would become an offer.
  5. A policy written by 1.6.3 or before says "conceptual units until the DeSciX DIP import exists" (plugin/templates/award-policy.md:3 at tag 1.6.3). The Offers import exists, and the template at 1.6.4 names it instead (section 3); init-repo.sh never rewrites a policy that is present (plugin/scripts/init-repo.sh:47), so such a policy keeps the old clause until its owner edits the Token: line.
  6. The public app repos carry no award policy or bounty template yet. Measured 2026-10-21 at 19:37 UTC: DeSciX/egpt-godsworld and DeSciX/egpt-frqtl each returned 404 for .beast and .github and had no bounty issue, open or closed, so award.sh has no published policy to cite there. Every bounty body says "see .beast/award-policy.md" (plugin/scripts/bounty.sh:32), a file contributors cannot read until it is published. DeSciX/EGPT carries .beast/awarders.json and no awards/ directory yet.
  7. The .md totals of decimal awards are floating-point sums. The per-login total adds the amounts with jq's numbers (plugin/scripts/ledger.sh:163): measured with jq 1.7.1, jq -n '[0.1,0.2]|add' prints 0.30000000000000004. Each award's own amount in the .jsonl, the .md list and the sheet is written as the award wrote it.
  8. Whether EGPT is a registered token with its community present on the platform was not measured here; the import refuses every row otherwise.
